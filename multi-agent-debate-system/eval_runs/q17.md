## Q17: A globally-distributed feature serves users on multiple continents. Should the team choose strong consistency with a single-region primary (simple correctness, but higher latency for distant users and a regional outage takes the feature down), or eventual consistency with multi-region replicas (low latency and high availability everywhere, but the application must handle conflicts and stale reads)?

- **Cross-examination rounds:** 6 · **History mode:** hybrid · **Elapsed:** 661s
- **Defence position:** The Defence will argue that strong consistency with a single-region primary is essential to ensure data correctness and simplicity in the application, even at the cost of higher latency for distant users and potential regional outages.
- **Prosecution position:** The Prosecution will argue that eventual consistency with multi-region replicas is necessary to provide low latency and high availability across all regions, despite the need to handle conflicts and tolerate stale reads in the application.

### Opening statements

**DEFENCE — opening statement**

Strong consistency achieved through a single-region primary ensures that every read reflects the most recent write, eliminating ambiguity for business logic and audit trails in a globally distributed feature. The simplicity of this model removes the need to implement complex conflict-resolution or merge logic, thereby reducing code complexity, maintenance overhead, and potential bugs that could compromise data integrity. Moreover, by keeping all transactional state in one place, the system can enforce strict ACID properties, guaranteeing that regulatory compliance, billing, and inventory counts remain accurate regardless of geographic location. Although distant users experience higher latency and a single regional failure temporarily disables the feature, those risks are outweighed by the predictable correctness and ease of debugging that come with strong consistency.

_Key points:_
- Data integrity and correctness for every user request
- Simplicity of application logic without conflict resolution
- Compliance with strict regulatory and audit requirements
- Guarantee of ACID properties in all transactions
- Reduced maintenance burden and bug surface area
- Predictable system behavior across all regions


**PROSECUTION — opening statement**

The Prosecution will argue that eventual consistency with multi-region replicas is the optimal choice for this globally-distributed feature, as it ensures low latency and high availability across all regions. By replicating data across multiple regions, users experience faster response times regardless of their location, enhancing overall user satisfaction. This approach also provides robust fault tolerance; even if one region experiences an outage, the system remains operational through other replicas, ensuring uninterrupted service. While managing conflicts and stale reads introduces complexity, these challenges are inherently manageable with careful application design and conflict resolution strategies, ultimately delivering a more resilient and scalable solution.
