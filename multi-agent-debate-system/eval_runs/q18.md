### Opening statements

**DEFENCE — opening statement**

The team should build an in‑house LLM gateway to maintain full control over routing, cost tracking, and auditability, ensuring long‑term flexibility and cost savings despite the initial development burden. By developing a proprietary gateway we gain granular insight into every token consumed, allowing dynamic allocation of cheaper models for noncritical workloads and premium ones for mission‑critical tasks, something that third‑party gateways cannot provide with the same precision. An internal solution also eliminates vendor lock‑in: we can swap providers without costly contractual renegotiations, adapt to new pricing tiers or emerging open‑source LLMs, and remain agile in a rapidly evolving AI landscape. Moreover, the gateway becomes an opportunity to implement advanced caching, latency reduction, and security hardening that directly reduce operational costs and comply with regulatory audit requirements—benefits that a black‑box vendor tool simply cannot match.

_Key points:_
- Granular cost tracking per token and per model, enabling dynamic budget allocation
- Full control over routing logic for optimal latency and reliability
- Auditability of every call for compliance and internal governance
- Avoidance of vendor lock‑in and dependency on external update cycles
- Opportunity to implement custom caching and security layers
- Long‑term cost savings outweigh initial development effort


**PROSECUTION — opening statement**

The court should rule in favor of adopting a vendor AI gateway due to its numerous advantages over building an in-house LLM gateway. This decision will allow the team to focus on core product development, benefit from vendor expertise and cost efficiency, while mitigating lock-in risks through strategic planning.

_Key points:_
- Immediate deployment saves time and resources compared to developing a new system.
- Leverages vendor expertise in AI infrastructure management.
- Potential for cost savings through optimized infrastructure use.
- Strategic contract terms can mitigate dependency risks.
