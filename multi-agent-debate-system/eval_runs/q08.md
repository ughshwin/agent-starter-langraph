## Q8: An autonomous agent will perform multi-step operations against production systems on a schedule. Should it hold broad standing credentials with heavy audit logging and anomaly detection (fewer blockers, faster, but a large blast radius if compromised or misbehaving), or scoped short-lived credentials with human approval required for any sensitive or irreversible action (much safer, but slower and operationally heavier)?

### Opening statements

**DEFENCE — opening statement**

The autonomous agent should utilize broad standing credentials with heavy audit logging and anomaly detection to ensure operational efficiency and minimize blockers, as the security risks are mitigated by robust monitoring and rapid response capabilities.

_Key points:_
- Broad standing credentials eliminate repetitive credential acquisition, reducing human latency in production workflows and ensuring continuous availability for scheduled operations. 
Heavy audit logging creates an immutable ledger that satisfies compliance mandates (e.g., SOC 2, ISO 27001) while providing forensic visibility that outstrips manual approval logs, thereby enabling rapid incident investigation and root‑cause analysis. 
Advanced anomaly detection leverages real‑time telemetry to detect deviations before escalation, allowing automated rollback or isolation with minimal human intervention, a feature impossible under short‑lived approvals where the operator must manually assess each action. 
Operational overhead is substantially lower: credential rotation, approval queues, and handoff delays are eliminated, freeing engineering capacity for higher‑value tasks such as feature innovation and system optimization. 
Cost savings accrue from reduced licensing of human‑centric workflow tools, fewer training cycles, and decreased risk‑management staffing needs because the agent’s actions remain auditable and traceable in a single consolidated log.


**PROSECUTION — opening statement**

The autonomous agent must employ scoped, short-lived credentials requiring human approval for sensitive actions to prioritize security and prevent catastrophic failures, even at the cost of operational speed and complexity. This approach aligns with the principle of least privilege, minimizing the potential blast radius in case of a compromise or malfunction. By necessitating human oversight, it ensures that any sensitive action is subject to scrutiny, reducing the risk of unauthorized operations. While this method introduces operational complexity and may slow down processes, the trade-off is justified by the enhanced security it provides, protecting against significant damages that could arise from unchecked access.

_Key points:_
- Scoped credentials reduce the potential impact of a breach by limiting system access.
- Human approval for sensitive actions adds a layer of oversight, preventing unintended operations.
- Least privilege principle minimizes risks associated with autonomous agent activities.
- Enhanced security measures outweigh the operational inefficiencies in critical production environments.
