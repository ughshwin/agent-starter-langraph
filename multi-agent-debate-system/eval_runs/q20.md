## Q20: Two internal services exchange high-volume requests and the team wants stronger contracts and lower overhead. Should they adopt gRPC for service-to-service calls (efficient binary protocol, strict schemas, streaming — but worse browser/debugging ergonomics and a steeper learning curve), or stay on REST/JSON (ubiquitous tooling, easy to inspect and debug — but looser contracts and more serialization overhead)?

- **Defence position:** The Defence will argue that adopting gRPC is the optimal choice because its efficient binary protocol, strict schema enforcement, and streaming capabilities directly address the need for stronger contracts and lower overhead, despite the trade-offs in browser ergonomics and learning curve.
- **Prosecution position:** The Prosecution will argue that retaining REST/JSON is preferable due to its ubiquitous tooling, ease of debugging, and developer familiarity, which outweigh the benefits of gRPC's efficiency and strict contracts in this high-volume internal service context.

### Opening statements

**DEFENCE — opening statement**

Adopting gRPC will deliver measurable gains in performance and reliability for high‑volume inter‑service traffic, directly satisfying the team’s objective of stronger contracts and lower overhead. The Protobuf binary format reduces serialization cost by roughly 30–50 % compared to JSON, cutting payload size and CPU cycles per request, which is critical when millions of messages are exchanged each day. Enforced schema definitions eliminate runtime type errors and provide automatic API documentation, guaranteeing that both parties adhere to the same contract without manual review or ad‑hoc validation logic. Native HTTP/2 support enables bi‑directional streaming, allowing the services to push incremental updates over a single connection—eliminating the need for costly polling loops and further reducing latency. The learning curve is outweighed by long‑term maintenance savings: generated stubs ensure consistency across languages, tooling integrates with CI/CD pipelines, and the strong typing prevents regressions that would otherwise require expensive runtime debugging.

_Key points:_
- Efficient binary serialization reduces payload size and CPU usage
- Strict schema enforcement guarantees contract compliance and automated documentation
- HTTP/2 streaming eliminates polling and lowers latency
- Generated code reduces maintenance effort and aligns team practices


**PROSECUTION — opening statement**

The Prosecution will argue that retaining REST/JSON is preferable due to its ubiquitous tooling, ease of debugging, and developer familiarity, which outweigh the benefits of gRPC's efficiency and strict contracts in this high-volume internal service context. While gRPC offers a more efficient binary protocol and streaming capabilities, the overhead of debugging with less tooling support and steeper learning curves makes it less practical for an internal team already comfortable with REST/JSON. The ability to quickly inspect and resolve issues using familiar tools like curl or Postman is crucial for maintaining productivity. Additionally, the flexibility and compatibility of REST/JSON ensure a broader range of applications and devices can be supported without vendor lock-in. These factors, combined with the current team's expertise in REST/JSON, make it a more sustainable choice that minimizes operational burden and maximizes efficiency in the long run.

_Key points:_
- Ubiquitous tooling support for REST/JSON facilitates easier debugging and rapid issue resolution.
- Familiarity with REST/JSON reduces onboarding time and training costs compared to gRPC's steeper learning curve.
- The flexibility of REST/JSON allows for a wider range of applications, reducing the risk of vendor lock-in.
- Ease of debugging tools (e.g., curl, Postman) are readily available, unlike the limited tooling for gRPC.
