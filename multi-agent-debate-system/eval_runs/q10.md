Should they adopt fully autonomous continuous deployment (every green pipeline ships straight to production, relying on tests, canaries, and fast rollback), or keep a manual approval gate before production (slower and a human bottleneck, but an explicit checkpoint that suits their light test coverage and occasional compliance review)?

### Opening statements

**DEFENCE — opening statement**

A 20‑engineer organization seeking to expedite feature delivery must adopt fully autonomous continuous deployment, because the combined strength of deterministic testing pipelines, progressive canary rollouts, and instant rollback procedures supplies a level of safety that rivals or surpasses any manual gate while eliminating human bottlenecks that slow time‑to‑market. By executing every green pipeline directly into production, developers receive immediate feedback on the impact of changes, enabling rapid iteration; this short feedback loop dramatically reduces the risk of costly post‑release defects and aligns with modern DevOps best practices proven across large‑scale distributed systems. Automated test suites—unit, integration, contract, and end‑to‑end—run at a scale and speed no human could match, and their results are recorded in immutable metrics dashboards that satisfy audit requirements; canary releases embed real‑world monitoring into the deployment flow, automatically throttling or aborting any rollout that violates predefined health thresholds. Finally, fast rollback mechanisms built into the release engine guarantee that any regressions are contained within seconds, protecting customer experience without relying on manual intervention, thus preserving operational continuity and reducing total cost of ownership.

_Key points:_
- Automated tests provide deterministic, repeatable safety nets that scale with code changes
- Progressive canary rollouts deliver real‑world monitoring and instant rollback at the edge of risk
- Human bottlenecks in manual gates become a source of latency, not safety, especially for a 20‑engineer team
- Fast rollback mechanisms eliminate post‑deployment incidents faster than any human review could
- Continuous deployment aligns with proven industry standards for high‑frequency releases and compliance verification


**PROSECUTION — opening statement**

The court should order the company to retain a manual approval gate before production deployment to ensure compliance and mitigate risks associated with their limited test coverage. By maintaining this checkpoint, the company ensures adherence to regulatory standards and allows skilled engineers to catch potential issues that automated tests might miss. While fully autonomous continuous deployment may offer speed, the current setup's vulnerabilities make it a risky proposition without proper safeguards. The manual gate acts as a critical safety net, balancing operational efficiency with necessary precautions to protect against outages and compliance lapses.

_Key points:_
- Ensures compliance by providing an explicit checkpoint for regulatory adherence.
- Mitigates risks from limited test coverage through human oversight.
- Serves as a safeguard against potential deployment issues that automated systems might overlook.
- Balances operational efficiency with necessary precautions to prevent outages and legal consequences.
