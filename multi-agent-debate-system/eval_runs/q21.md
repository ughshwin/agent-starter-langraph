## Q21: A read-heavy service is hitting database limits. Should the team add a Redis cache-aside layer in front of the database (big latency and load wins, but cache invalidation complexity and a risk of serving stale data), or scale reads with PostgreSQL read replicas (no application-level cache logic and strong-ish consistency, but replication lag, more database cost, and a lower ceiling than caching)?

- **Defence position:** The Defence will argue that implementing a Redis cache-aside layer is the optimal solution because it provides significant latency and load reduction, even at the cost of increased complexity in cache invalidation and the risk of serving stale data.
- **Prosecution position:** The Prosecution will argue that scaling with PostgreSQL read replicas is the superior approach because it eliminates application-level cache logic, maintains strong consistency, and avoids the long-term risks of stale data, despite higher costs and replication lag.

### Opening statements

**DEFENCE — opening statement**

The defence contends that adding a Redis cache‑aside layer is the optimal solution for this read‑heavy service because it delivers immediate and measurable reductions in latency and database load, thereby preventing future bottlenecks that would otherwise arise from scaling PostgreSQL replicas alone.
By keeping the most frequently accessed data in memory, the system can serve thousands of requests per second with sub‑10‑millisecond response times—an improvement unattainable through replica read scaling without incurring prohibitive infrastructure costs and replication lag. 
While cache invalidation does add application‑level complexity, established patterns such as write‑through or publish/subscribe on a message bus can enforce consistency within milliseconds, mitigating the stale‑data risk to an acceptable threshold for most business use cases.
Furthermore, the operational burden of managing Redis—auto‑scaling shards, monitoring evictions, and handling failover—is far less than maintaining a growing fleet of read replicas that must be provisioned, patched, and monitored across multiple regions, making the solution more maintainable at scale.

_Key points:_
- Significant latency reduction via in‑memory access
- Immediate database load relief without additional DB instances
- Established cache‑invalidation patterns keep staleness minimal
- Lower operational cost compared to scaling read replicas
- Easier scalability through sharding and auto‑scaling Redis clusters


**PROSECUTION — opening statement**

The Prosecution submits that scaling with PostgreSQL read replicas is the superior approach in this scenario. By leveraging read replicas, the application-level cache logic is eliminated, thereby reducing complexity and potential points of failure. This approach ensures strong data consistency across all replicas, minimizing the risk of serving stale or outdated information to users. While it is true that read replicas come with higher costs and replication lag, these trade-offs are outweighed by the benefits of maintaining data integrity and operational simplicity. The long-term stability and reliability provided by PostgreSQL read replicas make them a more sustainable solution compared to the risks associated with cache invalidation in a Redis-based approach.

_Key points:_
- Eliminates application-level cache logic, reducing complexity and potential points of failure.
- Ensures strong data consistency across all replicas, minimizing the risk of serving stale or outdated information.
- Avoids long-term risks such as cache invalidation issues and potential for stale data in Redis-based solutions.
