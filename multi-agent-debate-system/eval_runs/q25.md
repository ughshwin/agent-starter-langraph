Do you ship RBAC now to clear the audit, or invest in ReBAC and manage the timeline risk?

### Opening statements

RBAC, while imperfect for long‑term sharing semantics, can be implemented in a single sprint by leveraging existing libraries and the team's familiar stack, ensuring rapid delivery with minimal defect risk. Moreover, the audit’s primary focus is on documented controls, not on the sophistication of those controls; RBAC satisfies this requirement at low cost and with immediate compliance, allowing the company to avoid costly re‑audit or regulatory penalties that would far outweigh the short‑term inconvenience of a less precise model.

_Key points:_
- RBAC delivers required access control within 10 weeks using existing developer skills
- Audit focuses on documented controls, not fine granularity—RBAC suffices for compliance
- Implementing ReBAC/ABAC risks delay, technical debt, and audit failure due to team inexperience
- Transition to a more granular model can be scheduled post‑audit without disrupting current operations


**PROSECUTION — opening statement**

The fintech must prioritize implementing a fine-grained relationship/attribute-based access control system (ReBAC/ABAC) such as OpenFGA. While this approach presents technical challenges and risks delaying compliance with the upcoming audit, its long-term benefits far outweigh the immediate concerns. The product's sharing model requires precise access control, which RBAC cannot provide effectively, necessitating a future replacement if RBAC is chosen now. By investing in ReBAC/ABAC, the company avoids the costly and labor-intensive process of overhauling an incompatible system, ensuring scalability and adaptability as the product evolves. This strategic decision also fosters team growth by leveraging existing expertise and building internal capabilities, ultimately enhancing operational efficiency and resilience against future challenges.

_Key points:_
- Long-term alignment with the product's sharing model requires fine-grained access control, which ReBAC/ABAC provides effectively.
- Cost savings over time as RBAC would necessitate a complete overhaul later, incurring higher long-term costs.
- ReBAC/ABAC offers scalability and flexibility, crucial for adapting to evolving product needs and market demands.
- Investing in team capability through training and leadership opportunities improves morale and future problem-solving capacity.
